## 1. For each app: core value proposition/methodology, pricing, user/subscriber/revenue numbers, funding

### Takeaway
The market has split into (a) premium subscription "financial dashboards" built on bank aggregation (Monarch ~$100/yr, Copilot ~$95-109/yr, YNAB $109/yr), (b) a mass-market freemium "savings utility" (Rocket Money, ~5M paying subscribers), and (c) low-priced, manual-first global trackers (Money Manager, Wallet, Spendee, Toshl, Goodbudget at roughly $2-6/month). Mint, the ad/lead-gen-funded free incumbent, was shut down in March 2024 and its user base was the main growth fuel for the subscription players.

### Cited Findings

**YNAB (You Need A Budget) — zero-based "give every dollar a job" method, subscription only**

- Size signals are weak and inconsistent: old podcast interviews cite ~$4.5-5M revenue (pre-SaaS era, date unclear) — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/?p=2175); headcount estimates range from ~125 to ~200 — [Built In Boston](https://www.builtinboston.com/company/ynab), [Zippia](https://www.zippia.com/you-need-a-budget-careers-1554981).

### Inferences
- Price ladder as of 2026: premium aggregators cluster at ~$95-110/year (YNAB, Monarch, Copilot, Origin); mid-market ~$48-80/year (Simplifi, Goodbudget Plus, EveryDollar annual low quote); global manual-first trackers ~$15-36/year (Spendee Plus, Money Manager sync, Toshl). The Mongolian target of ~$0.70/month (~$8.40/year) sits below even the cheapest global tier, which is consistent with local purchasing power but implies volume, not ARPU, must carry the business.
- Rocket Money (~5M payers) and Cleo (1.3M payers) are an order of magnitude larger by paying users than the "pure budgeting" apps (Monarch ~0.5M, Copilot ~0.1M). Both sell an immediate cash outcome (cancel a subscription, get an advance) rather than "budgeting"; pure budgeting remains a niche that monetises well per user but not in volume.
- Sliding-scale "pay what's fair" pricing (Rocket Money) is a notable mechanism for markets with wide income dispersion.

## 2. How do they acquire users, and what drives retention?

### Takeaway
The winning acquisition pattern is organic: community word-of-mouth (Reddit), methodology/content and a trigger event (Mint's shutdown), supported by long free trials; retention comes from setup investment (linked accounts, rules, categories), annual billing, and habit formation — with goal-setting in the first days being the best-evidenced lever.

### Cited Findings
- Monarch: the Mint shutdown notice spread first through Reddit, where Monarch became the default recommendation; it had pre-built Mint import tools; new-user arrivals doubled immediately and subscribers grew 20x over the following year — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/ca1c5148-1918-4484-a521-28aec612ac83/). Monarch also published a migration page with a discount for Mint users, written by Mint's former first product manager — [Monarch](https://www.monarch.com/mint-shutting-down-how-to-migrate-to-monarch-with-discount).
- Monarch retention per Sacra: comes from "making setup worth it" — linking accounts, fixing categories, building rules, tracking goals; annual billing turns onboarding effort into a retention asset/lock-in — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/5505a084-d3d2-4bec-98d3-ec019cbc7154/), [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/b29b7251-f3d5-4a9b-b7bd-0ce8d9437d3b/).
- YNAB: long 34-day trial, free year for students, method-led positioning; uses lifecycle messaging (Braze) to drive high-value actions, revenue and retention — [The Penny Hoarder](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/budgeting/ynab-review/), [Braze case study](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/how-you-need-a-budget-drives-high-value-actions-and-revenue-with-braze.md). Community resources and live classes are cited as a retention factor (weak, AI-generated source) — [marlvel.ai](https://marlvel.ai/apps/ynab).
- Rocket Money: referral programme for Premium; distribution through parent Rocket Companies (free Premium for mortgage clients); the 10-K (filed 2 March 2026) coverage says the unified Rocket brand restage and heavier marketing spend lifted Rocket Money subscriber counts — [Rocket Money referral terms](https://www.rocketmoney.com/referral-terms-conditions), [Rocket Money Help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/6396483-i-m-a-rocket-mortgage-client-do-i-get-rocket-money-for-free), [Blockonomi](https://blockonomi.com/rocket-companies-rkt-stock-plunges-7-7-following-disappointing-2025-earnings-report/).
- Appfigures (Nov 2023): since Jan 2020 Mint's monthly downloads fell 42% while Rocket Money's rose 41% — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20231103?f=3).
- Copilot: majority of its 100k+ subscribers open the app daily (company claim via review) — [WalletGrower](https://walletgrower.com/blog/copilot-money-review-2026).
- EveryDollar acquires through the Ramsey media/personality ecosystem (product is the tool for Ramsey's plan) — [The Penny Hoarder](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/budgeting/best-budgeting-apps/?aff_id=401).
- WeMoney (Australia) via Amplitude: users who set money goals in their first three days had higher 12-month retention; building goal-setting into onboarding raised retention 20%, and lookalike audiences + personalised messaging halved CAC — [Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/blog/increasing-retention-wemoney.md).
- Finance is described as a habit-driven category that keeps new app users returning longer than other industries — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/habit-driven-categories-like-finance-keep-new-app-users-coming-back), [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/finance-apps-keep-users-engaged-longer-than-any-other-industry).
- Trial design benchmarks (all subscription apps, RevenueCat 2026): trials of 17-32 days convert at a median 42.5% vs 25.5% for trials under 4 days; over half of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on day zero — [RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026), [heise](https://heise.de/-11310636).

### Inferences
- No leader relied primarily on paid ads to build its base; each had an owned distribution asset: a method and teaching content (YNAB), a media personality (EveryDollar/Ramsey), a parent company's customer base (Rocket), or a community moment (Monarch/Reddit). A small Mongolian app's equivalent is likely local Facebook groups/creators and a locally resonant method or challenge rather than app-store ads.
- "Be ready for the switching event" is a transferable lesson: Monarch won because import tooling existed before Mint's shutdown. Locally, an import path from spreadsheets or from any competing/local bank-app export is a cheap acquisition asset.
- Retention in aggregation apps comes from sunk setup effort and data history; a manual-entry app has to manufacture the same lock-in via accumulated history, shared family groups (multi-user switching cost) and goals set during onboarding.

### Gaps
- No sourced breakdown of paid vs organic acquisition spend or CAC for any of these apps.
- YNAB's specific channel mix (YouTube, workshops, podcast, subreddit) is widely discussed but was not confirmed by a primary source in this session.
- Influencer/affiliate economics: most "best budgeting app" review sites are affiliate-funded (visible in URLs with affiliate parameters), suggesting affiliate marketing is a major channel for Monarch/Rocket/YNAB, but no payout figures were found.
- Company-reported churn/retention rates for YNAB, Monarch, Copilot, Rocket Money: none published.

## 3. What business models work vs failed? Freemium conversion and retention benchmarks

### Takeaway
Free, ad/lead-gen-funded budgeting (Mint) failed on unit economics and incentive conflict; subscription works for the committed niche (YNAB, Monarch, Copilot), while the largest businesses pair a subscription with an immediate monetary outcome or a credit product (Rocket Money, Cleo). Benchmarks: freemium converts roughly 2% of downloads to paid versus ~10-11% for hard paywalls, with near-identical one-year retention (~27-28% for annual plans), and finance apps retain only ~3-7% of installs at day 30.

### Cited Findings
- Mint monetised via referral fees from financial institutions (credit cards, loans, investment accounts) plus targeted ads — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/how-does-mint-make-money/). Sacra puts Mint's ARPU at ~$15/year — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/994b0213-77a8-4d0d-a47f-a96042526383/). Appfigures' mobile-only estimate was $1.6M net revenue in 2023 YTD (~$0.86 per user) — a different metric (in-app revenue only) — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20231103?f=3).
- Why Mint struggled (analyst/blog consensus, not an official Intuit explanation): low revenue per user against rising data-aggregation costs per active user; conflict of interest (recommending new credit cards to people trying to get out of debt); stagnant product and broken bank connections; and after Intuit bought Credit Karma (~130M users) Mint's standalone economics mattered little — [Tearsheet postmortem](https://tearsheet.co/banking/postmortem-did-credit-karma-kill-mint-or-was-pfm-already-on-life-support/), [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/34dd32f7-2f91-4a39-82b6-99ede69b4e95/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/02/personal-finance-monarch-intuit-mint).
- NBC/CNBC framing of the shutdown: "this is the peril of relying on free services" — [NBC](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/business/money-report/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-to-the-disappointment-of-loyal-users-this-is-the-peril-of-relying-on-free-services/3262734).
- Monarch explicitly chose subscription over ads/referrals; Sacra notes paid budgeting "demands product quality" — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/994b0213-77a8-4d0d-a47f-a96042526383/), [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/7c20b762-6f47-4f2d-8927-f4ef34a5ba2e/).
- Cleo: subscription is the minority of revenue (2025: $90M subscriptions vs $157M cash-advance express fees) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/7e293700-b63d-43b8-abf6-ab454f8a7beb/).
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (all categories): median day-35 download-to-paid conversion 10.7% for hard-paywall apps vs 2.1% for freemium; one-year retention of yearly subscribers 27% (hard paywall) vs 28% (freemium) — [RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026).
- First-year retention of annual plans fell from 31% to 28% YoY; monthly-plan 12-month retention fell from 10% to 8% — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-2026-insights-part-2). 95% of cancelling annual subscribers never return — [heise](https://heise.de/-11310636).
- Lower-priced apps retain better: 36% median vs 23% for high-priced apps — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights).
- Regional monetisation: median monthly revenue per payer $32 in North America, $25 in Western Europe, $14 in India/SE Asia; D60 revenue per install and year-1 LTV are 2.5-5x higher in North America than in the weakest regions — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/), [podcast summary](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2432582/episodes/18833665-insights-from-revenuecat-s-2026-state-of-subscription-apps-report).
- Install retention for finance apps: AppsFlyer-derived figures of ~6.6% (iOS) and ~3.1% (Android) at day 30; another compilation gives 4.6% D30; Adjust cross-category median ~4% D30 with 5-8% for strong performers; an outlier compilation cites fintech D1 22-30%, D7 17.6%, D30 11.6% (treat with caution) — secondary compilations: [Growth-onomics](https://growth-onomics.com/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks-by-industry-2026/), [UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/), [Lovable guide](https://lovable.dev/guides/what-is-a-good-retention-rate-for-an-app). Mixpanel publishes a finance-specific 2026 benchmark (not read) — [Mixpanel](https://mixpanel.com/blog/finance-benchmarks-2026/).

### Inferences
- For a freemium manual-entry app, a planning assumption of ~2% download-to-paid (RevenueCat freemium median) and ~3-7% D30 install retention is defensible; anything above is upside. At ~$0.70/month and 2% conversion, 10,000 downloads yield roughly 200 payers ≈ $140/month before store fees — the model needs either very large download volume, higher-priced annual/family tiers, or a secondary revenue line.
- Mint's failure does not prove that all non-subscription revenue fails; it shows that lead-gen cannot cover per-user aggregation costs. A manual-entry app has near-zero marginal data cost, so the cost side of Mint's problem does not apply — but the incentive-conflict and trust problem of promoting loans inside a budgeting tool does.
- Low price improves retention (36% vs 23%), which supports a very low local price point, and annual plans retain about 3x better than monthly over 12 months (28% vs 8%), which argues for offering and promoting an annual plan even at a tiny price.
- Emerging-market payers yield less than half of North American revenue per payer even on global apps, so global-app pricing should not be used as a reference for Mongolia.

### Gaps
- Finance-category-specific breakouts from RevenueCat's 2026 report (the PDF has by-category sections) were not accessible; all conversion/retention numbers above are cross-category.
- No sourced freemium-to-paid conversion rate for any individual budgeting app (YNAB trial conversion, Rocket Money free-to-Premium, Wallet/Spendee free-to-paid).
- The AppsFlyer/Adjust retention figures were seen only via secondary compilations; the exact primary report and methodology were not verified, and none isolate budgeting apps from banking/payment apps (which inflate "finance" engagement).

## 5. Which apps work without bank connections, and how do they keep manual-entry users engaged?

### Takeaway
Manual entry is the free/default mode of most global trackers (Money Manager, Wallet, Spendee, Toshl, Goodbudget, EveryDollar free) and is fully supported by YNAB; these apps keep manual users through a strong method or ritual (envelopes, zero-based), speed of entry, shared household ledgers, reminders/streaks/widgets, and by gating sync/backup or multi-device rather than core logging.

### Cited Findings
- Manual entry is free in Money Manager, Wallet, Spendee and Toshl; bank sync and multi-device/cloud features sit behind paid tiers — [Guiding Tech](https://www.guidingtech.com/best-android-apps-for-budgeting-in-2026/), [PocketClear](https://pocketclear.app/blog/budget-app-comparison-chart-2026.html), [SpotSaaS](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/spendee/pricing), [BudgetFlowHQ](https://budgetflowhq.pages.dev/posts/toshl-vs-wallet-budgeting-app-comparison/).
- Money Manager (Realbyte): 20M+ downloads on a manual-entry product whose main paid item is cloud sync (~$2.49/month) — [Guiding Tech](https://www.guidingtech.com/best-android-apps-for-budgeting-in-2026/).
- Goodbudget: manual-first envelope method, "no bank sync required", free tier syncs 2 devices for household sharing; envelope cap is the main upgrade trigger and a known complaint — [NerdWallet](https://nerdwallet.com/finance/learn/best-budget-apps), [The Penny Hoarder](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/budgeting/best-budgeting-apps/?aff_id=401).
- EveryDollar free tier is a manual zero-based budget; automation (bank sync) is the paid upgrade — [PocketClear](https://pocketclear.app/blog/budget-app-fees-explained.html).
- Spendee free tier limits structure (1 cash wallet, 1 budget) rather than number of entries — [SpotSaaS](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/spendee/pricing).
- Engagement mechanics seen in manual-only apps: daily logging reminders, streaks with weekly "forgiveness tokens", streak-saving alerts — [LifeKit Finance](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6758224496); streaks, no-spend days and recurring-bill reminders — [Budgey](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6503190110); home/lock-screen widgets, positioning for users uneasy about linking bank accounts (budget widgets premium) — [MoneyNote](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id1320730220).
- Onboarding goal-setting within the first three days correlates with higher 12-month retention (WeMoney/Amplitude) — [Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/blog/increasing-retention-wemoney.md).
- Privacy/control is an explicit selling point for manual apps; conversely aggregators carry privacy concerns (e.g., a 2026 article titled "Is Rocket Money safe? Secure, yes. Private, less so") — [Recurdash](https://recurdash.com/blog/is-rocket-money-safe).

### Inferences (lessons for a Mongolian manual-entry, family-group, ~2,500 MNT/month freemium app)
- The dominant paywall pattern for manual apps is: unlimited (or generous) logging free; charge for sync/backup, multi-device or shared access, more budgets/wallets/envelopes, and advanced reports. Limiting the number of transactions is rare among the leaders. For a family-centred app, the natural paid line is group size or number of shared budgets, while keeping at least 2-person sharing free may be needed for virality (Goodbudget's free 2-device sync is the precedent).
- Money Manager's scale shows a manual product can reach tens of millions of downloads at a ~$2.50/month price for sync — the closest global analogue to the planned product and price.
- Without bank sync, the daily habit is the product: fast entry (widget, quick-add, recurring transactions), a reminder at a consistent time, streaks with forgiveness, and a weekly/monthly summary that rewards the effort. Method-led framing (envelopes / zero-based / a local "salary-day plan") gives users a reason to log beyond record-keeping.
- Low price + annual plan is supported by benchmarks (low-priced apps retain 36% vs 23%; annual 28% vs monthly 8% at 12 months); expect ~2% freemium conversion and low single-digit D30 install retention as baselines.
- Avoid Mint's path: do not depend on lending/credit referral revenue as the core model in a trust-sensitive budgeting tool; if partner offers are added later, keep them clearly separate from budgeting advice.
- Distribution lesson: leaders grew via community and a teachable method, not ads — locally that likely means Mongolian-language content, Facebook groups and creator partnerships; and being ready with import tools when users switch from spreadsheets or other apps.

### Gaps
- No sourced retention or engagement data comparing manual-entry users with bank-synced users for any app.
- No primary-source detail on how Money Manager, Wallet, Spendee or Toshl specifically drive manual-entry engagement (notification strategy, streaks, widgets); the engagement mechanics cited come from smaller indie apps' store listings (self-described).
- No data found on budgeting-app performance in markets without open banking that are comparable to Mongolia (e.g., Central Asia), nor on willingness to pay at sub-$1/month price points.
- Honeydue's and Goodbudget's manual-vs-sync behaviour is described inconsistently across review sites; verify on vendor sites.
